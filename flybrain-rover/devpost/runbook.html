The fly's escape circuit, running here

150 neurons and 900 synapses of the male Drosophila connectome: LC4 and LPLC2, the looming detectors, converging on the giant fibre, the single neuron whose spike launches an escape jump. This is the same fixed-point arithmetic that runs on the Hacker Badge, at 100 Hz. Swat it. Then remove the eye and swat it again.

Every spike you see travels a measured synapse. There is no flinch rule in the code: press B and the eye still fires, but nothing reaches the giant fibre, because the only thing removed was the wiring out of it.

MLH: Best Use of Tiger Data live

The move
The wall chart. Every frame the brain produces streams into TimescaleDB hypertables as it happens, a continuous aggregate rolls it up per second, and the chart on the wall polls that aggregate once a second. Lesion the fly and the judge watches the steering trace fall to zero in the database, not just on the robot.
Judge sees
Three live traces (eye, steering, escape) with "newest bucket 1.6 s ago" and a row counter climbing. Then, in Tiger's own SQL editor: 
-- the steering asymmetry, straight out of the database
select time_bucket('2 seconds', t), grp, avg(hz)
  from neuron_rates
 where grp in ('DNa02_L','DNa02_R')
 group by 1,2;
Run it
cd ~/Downloads/filess/flybrain-rover
# terminal 4: stream the demo's frames into Tiger, one batch a second, until Ctrl-C
.venv/bin/python scripts/export_timeseries.py --live ws://localhost:8765 --run-id demo_$(date +%H%M)

# terminal 5: the wall chart, reads the continuous aggregate; open http://localhost:8790
.venv/bin/python scripts/live_chart.py

# the four recorded episodes are already loaded; this prints the table plus aggregate and compression stats
.venv/bin/python scripts/export_timeseries.py --report
The line
"Fifty hertz of neural data, 60,000 rows an episode, a quarter million rows loaded. It goes in by COPY a second at a time, a continuous aggregate keeps per-second rates fresh with the newest second computed on read, and compression kicks in after two minutes so a free instance holds a day of the robot. The lesion we just did is already in the time series."
Caveat
Cloud is downstream of the loop, never in it: the brain does not know the database exists. If the venue Wi-Fi drops, the demo runs on; only the chart stops updating.

The two-minute script

Lead with the controls, not the chase. The chase is only the readout that makes a lesion visible, and nobody has to be chased for the point to land.

If asked "why not just train a neural net": a net with random weights does nothing, and a trained one cannot be lesioned into a named cell type and predicted. Ours can. We predicted that removing one DNa02 would bias the turn to the other side before we ran it, and it did.

Graded LC10a lesion, person left: 0% removed gives 158.4 Hz, then 137.5, 83.4, 40.3, and 0.0 as quarters come out. The turn command saturates while the drive degrades, which is the point: the population is redundant, so half of it still steers.

When it breaks

  • Robot misbehaves or Wi-Fi drops. Press space for E-stop, add --dry-run, and narrate against the wall viz. The brain, retina and hotkeys all still run on the webcam.
  • No robot at all. replay/viewer.html plays the four recorded episodes with the same rover, retina, rates and spike raster.
  • Detector misses the judge. Stand at 1.5 m, or run --show to see the boxes. --fake-boxes proves the brain chain if the detector is the problem.
  • Latency over 100 ms. --yolo-device mps, --substeps 10, close the viz tab.
  • Nothing works at all. Hand them the badge. It needs no laptop, no network, and no robot.
Volunteer these before a judge finds them. The robot has never been driven on a real chassis; the bridge runs end to end in dry run at 15 ms. The gimbal, camera and blaster boards are offline, chassis and vision answer. There is no recorded video yet. And the AOTU pathway result says where the synaptic weight sits, not what the behaviour needs: we lesioned it and tracking survived at 61% against 71% for a size-matched random lesion.
