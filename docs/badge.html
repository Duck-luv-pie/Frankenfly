Put a fly's brain on your badge.

This is not a model of a fly and not a trained network. It is 150 neurons and 900 synapses copied from the wiring diagram of a real fruit fly, running on the badge in your lanyard. Swat at it and the neuron that launches a real fly's escape jump fires. One paste, no account, it stays on the badge after the event.

Try it here first

The same circuit, the same arithmetic, running in your browser. Swat it, then press B to cut the eye's wiring and swat again. There is no flinch rule anywhere; the escape only ever comes out of the synapses.

Install it on your badge

Four steps, about two minutes. You need Chrome or Edge and a USB cable that carries data.

copied, 16 KB, paste it in step 3
1

Battery switch off, then plug in

The switch is on the top edge. Use a cable that carries data, not a charge-only one. The badge shows up as a serial device.

2

Open the badge IDE

badge.hackthenorth.com/ide in Chrome or Edge. Safari and Firefox do not support WebSerial, so the Connect button will do nothing there.

3

Import app, paste, Connect, Push

Click Import app and paste what you copied above, the whole thing including the --[==[badge-app header at the top. Click Connect and pick the port named USB JTAG/serial debug unit. Click Push and keep the cable in until it finishes.

4

Open FlyBadge from the launcher, and swat

Switch the battery back on. FlyBadge is in your app grid. Swat at it: all six LEDs flash white and the fly jumps. B removes the eye's wiring, A puts it back, UP and DOWN set how hard a swat has to be. If it flinches on its own sitting on the table, press DOWN a few times.

What is actually in it

150
neurons, each a named cell in the connectome
900
synapses, with their measured counts and signs
100 Hz
membrane update, in integer arithmetic
0
lines of hand-written escape logic

In 2026 the complete wiring diagram of a male fruit fly's brain was published: every neuron, every synapse. Two cell types in the fly's eye, LC4 and LPLC2, detect an object looming toward the fly. They converge on one neuron, the giant fibre, whose single spike launches the escape jump. That is the circuit that fires when you swat at a fly and miss.

FlyBadge takes those cells and the synapses between them straight from the connectome, quantizes the synapse counts to fit in the badge's 48 KB of memory, and simulates them as leaky integrate-and-fire neurons on the badge's ESP32-C3. The badge has no light sensor, so the swat itself is the stimulus: the accelerometer sees your hand arrive, and that current goes into LC4 and LPLC2. Everything after that is the fly's own wiring.

It is a subsample. The full population is 311 cells and 20,607 synapses, which will not compile on the badge. The cells kept are the ones that project most strongly toward the giant fibre, and the file says so on its first line. The same arithmetic runs on a laptop in Python and in this page, and the three agree spike for spike.
