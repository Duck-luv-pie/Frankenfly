Three numbers, each smaller than the last

166,700
every neuron in a male fruit fly, from the connectome published this month
15,000
the ones on a path from eye to leg. That is what runs above, at 2,334,959 signed synapses
275
the LC10a tracking cells. Under two percent. Pull them and it goes blind to people

Switch to LC10a cut above. Every other neuron, every synapse and every weight is identical; all that changes is that every synapse onto and out of those 275 cells is set to zero. Watch what that does, because it is not what most people expect: LC10a still fires whenever somebody is in view, peaking at seventy hertz, because the retina injects current into it directly and the eye is not what we cut. It simply has nowhere to send it. DNa02 sits at zero on both sides for the whole fifteen seconds, and the rover wanders.

What you are looking at

The wedge is the fly's field of view, 98.4° wide, split into the 24 retina columns the brain actually reads. Each one lights with the fraction of it a person covers. That is the entire sensory input — the brain never sees a pixel.

DNa02 is the steering cell. There is one per side, and the difference between them is the turn command. Watch the two tubes trade places as a person crosses the field of view. Left eye, left steering neuron, and no line of code in between. On the lesioned run both sit at zero for the whole fifteen seconds.

The raster is 512 neurons sampled at random out of the fifteen thousand, flashing when they spike. It is a 20 millisecond window, so a dot means at least one spike in that step.

What is the fly's and what is ours

The steering is the claim, and it is untouched wiring: connection for connection, sign for sign, strength for strength, straight out of neuPrint. Three things are ours and we label them everywhere. The encoder that turns a camera into current on the eye neurons. The read-out that turns descending-neuron firing rates into wheel commands. The search behaviour for when nobody is in view, which is a state we wrote, not a circuit we found.

And one caveat worth saying out loud. This page is a recording, not a live simulation — fifteen thousand leaky integrate-and-fire neurons will not run in a browser tab. It was produced by the same code in the repository, at 50 Hz, and the replay files are checked in so you can regenerate them yourself.
